Research

My research centers on the mechanisms of visual cognition — visual working memory, visual attention, and object recognition. I am especially interested in how the meaning we attach to objects interacts with our ability to actively hold visual information in mind.

My PhD work was focused on why meaningful, real-world objects are remembered better than abstract visual stimulil, and on showing that this benefit reflects genuine semantic understanding rather than surface familiarity or verbal labeling. To study these questions I combine psychophysics with EEG.

Background

I received my B.S. in Cognitive and Behavioral Neuroscience from UC San Diego, where I worked in the labs of Dr. Tim Brady, Dr. John Serences, and Dr. Viola Störmer on projects investigating visual working memory, object tracking, and how visual attention influences perception. I went on to complete my PhD at Dartmouth College with Dr. Viola Störmer, and since 2026 I have continued in the same lab as a postdoctoral researcher.

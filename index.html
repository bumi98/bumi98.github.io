Research

My research centers on the mechanisms of visual cognition — visual working memory, visual attention, and object recognition. I am especially interested in how the meaning we attach to objects interacts with our ability to actively hold visual information in mind.

A recurring theme in my work is that meaningful, real-world objects are remembered better than simple features alone, and that this benefit reflects genuine semantic understanding rather than surface familiarity or verbal labeling. To study these questions I combine psychophysics with neuroimaging, primarily EEG.
